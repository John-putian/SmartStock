# How each role uses Smart Stock

Plain language, in the order the work actually happens. Four roles, four jobs.

---

## Work Staff — receives the container and lists what is in it

1. **Sign in** on a phone or a computer. The floor dashboard opens: pieces on
   the floor, what is running low, and your own requests.
2. **Inventory → Add item.**
3. **Step 1, the photo.** Tap **Take photo** for the rear camera, or **Choose
   from gallery** for a picture already on the phone. The photo is optional and
   you can type everything by hand instead. This is a *picture of the product* —
   it is not a QR scan.
4. If photo suggestions have been switched on, tap **Suggest details from
   photo**. Nothing is sent anywhere until you press that button. A draft name,
   category and description come back; tick only the ones you want and press
   **Use selected details**. Everything stays editable. If suggestions are not
   switched on, the button says so and you type the details yourself.
5. **Step 2, the details.** Name, category, condition, description. Condition is
   yours to judge from the actual piece — nothing guesses it for you.
6. **Step 3, price and quantity.** Enter what the shop paid (acquisition cost)
   and what it will sell for. **Suggest initial price** looks at what similar
   items actually sold for here and offers a figure; if nothing matches it
   offers ₱500 clearly marked as a demo placeholder. It only fills the field
   when you press **Use suggested price**, and it never touches acquisition
   cost. Quantity is how many physical pieces arrived.
7. **More details** stays shut unless you need brand, SKU, serial number or
   reorder level. Leave SKU blank and the system makes one.
8. **Save product.** The button sits at the bottom of the screen the whole time.
   If something is wrong you come back to the same form with your typing intact
   and the problem written beside the field — except the photo, which browsers
   will not hold on to, so you choose it again.
9. **Print labels for stock** on the item screen, then stick **one label on each
   physical piece**. Every piece gets its own code.

**Changing a price later:** you may raise a listed price yourself. Lowering one
becomes a request — write why, and the item keeps its old price until a manager
or the administrator agrees.

---

## Cashier — sells

1. **Sign in.** The till is the cashier's station; nobody else can open it.
2. **Add pieces to the sale**, two ways:
   - **Scan the QR label.** The scan box reads *typed characters* — a USB or
     Bluetooth scanner works like a keyboard and types the code for you. Smart
     Stock never uses a camera to read codes. With no scanner, type the code and
     press Enter.
   - **Tap the item's card**, which takes the oldest piece of that item.
   A piece code (`AA-FUR-0007-03`) sells that exact piece. An item SKU
   (`AA-FUR-0007`) takes the oldest one. An unknown code flashes red and adds
   nothing.
3. **Need to take money off?** Type the percentage and the reason and send it.
   The sale stands still at full price until someone answers.
4. When it comes back approved, the discount shows on the cart with the name of
   whoever approved it. **Complete Sale & Print Receipt.** Stock comes off the
   moment the sale is recorded.
5. If the sale changed after the discount was approved, the system stops you and
   says so. Ask again on the sale as it now stands.

---

## Store Manager — decides

1. **Sign in.** The office dashboard: today's takings, the month, stock on hand,
   alerts.
2. **Price Approvals** holds two different things:
   - **Discount requests** — a till is waiting right now. Deal with these first.
     **You can decide these alone — you do not need the owner.** The owner can
     also approve them.
   - **Price requests** — a change to a shelf price, which can wait. You or the
     administrator can decide these.
3. Approving a discount lets that one till take that percentage off **that one
   sale, once**. Approving a price writes the new price onto the item.
4. Either way your name goes on the decision and into the audit trail.
5. **Reports** and **Sales Analytics** are yours: sales and revenue, inventory
   valuation, stock movement, what sold by category, and the forecast.

---

## Administrator (the owner) — sees everything

1. Everything the manager has, plus **User Accounts** and **Mail settings**.
2. **You see every transaction** as it happens, and the audit trail records who
   did what, from sign-ins to refused attempts.
3. You are told when a discount is asked for and again when it is actually used,
   naming the cashier and the approver.
4. **You can authorise a mark-down on your own goods.** A discount asked for at
   the till can be approved by you or by the Store Manager — whoever is nearer.
   The manager does not need you, and you do not need the manager. Your name goes
   on the sale and into the audit trail either way. The cashier and work staff
   can never approve one.

---

## What everyone shares

- **Several people can be signed in at once**, on different machines — the shop
  genuinely works that way. One browser holds one sign-in, so use a second
  browser or a private window to be two people on one laptop.
- **Stock is counted, never typed.** An item's quantity is worked out by counting
  the pieces still in stock, so the number on screen cannot drift from the shelf.
- **Two tills cannot sell the same piece.** Whoever finishes first gets it; the
  other is told.
- **Every consequential action is written to the audit trail** with a name on it.
